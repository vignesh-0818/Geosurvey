Understanding Property Boundaries Before Buying Land

  1. Home
  2. Blog
  3. Boundary Surveys
May 14, 2026 Dr. James Mitchell Boundary Surveys

Purchasing land is one of the most substantial financial commitments an individual or business will ever make. However, many buyers assume that visual landmarks—such as existing fences, tree lines, or paved driveways—accurately reflect legal property boundaries. In reality, relying on assumptions rather than an up-to-date boundary survey frequently leads to costly disputes and title encumbrances.

Why Visual Markers Can Be Deceptive

Over decades of ownership, previous proprietors often install fences where construction was easiest rather than along the exact deed line. Without a certified survey, you may unknowingly purchase a parcel where a neighbor's shed encroaches onto your land, or where your intended driveway encroaches on municipal right-of-way.

Critical Due Diligence Steps Before Closing

Conducting professional boundary verification during your inspection contingency period gives you leverage and security:

  • Pin Recovery and Monument Verification: Surveyors locate original iron boundary monuments or set new certified pins conforming to official county records.
  • Easement and Setback Identification: Unrecorded utility corridors, drainage pathways, or access rights can severely limit where you can build.
  • Discrepancy Reconciliation: Comparing the legal deed description against actual physical occupation prevents boundary litigation before closing title.

Protecting Your Real Estate Investment

A certified boundary plat signed and sealed by a Licensed Professional Surveyor (PLS) is a permanent legal document recognized by mortgage underwriters, title insurers, and courts. Having this documentation in hand ensures peace of mind for future improvements, fencing, and resale value.
